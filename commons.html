How to read Commons

Every instrument in Commons moves through three stages:

  • Indexed: Commons knows the instrument exists and where its official text is published.
  • Sourced: the official text is deposited in Commons, with its licence and provenance.
  • Encoded: some or all of its rules are written in L4, with worked cases that test them.

Each jurisdiction has its own page and statute map. The addresses follow one pattern: /commons/<jurisdiction>/<type>/<name>, for example /commons/au-wa/act/wills-act-1970. A jurisdiction is a country code (au, sg, nz) or a country and state code (au-wa), the form the Akoma Ntoso legislative standard uses; Commonwealth law is at /commons/au, Western Australian law at /commons/au-wa.

Contribute

Encode a provision, verify an encoding against its source, or nominate a statute.

Every encoding in Commons is a draft, and none has yet been verified against its source by a named reviewer. First-pass encodings are written by an AI agent in a single pass from the deposited text and checked only by their own worked cases. The figures show how much of the statute book is in executable form, not how much is verified. The library is open source at github.com/legalese/commons.
