World map

One row for each country, and for the states, provinces and territories of the United States, Canada and Australia and for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. A row is that jurisdiction's statute book: its Acts in force laid end to end, each as wide as its pages, on the same scale for every row. Blue Acts are encoded in L4; the darker the blue, the more of the Act's sections are encoded. Grey Acts are in Commons but not yet encoded. The pale block at the end of a row stands for the Acts Commons does not hold yet. Subsidiary legislation is left out.

Hover over a row for its figures. Click a jurisdiction that has a page here to open it, or one of its encoded Acts to open the Act. On a phone, tap once for the figures and again to open.

A page is a page of text, 2,600 characters, in every row, so the rows can be compared. The shade of blue is counted section by section for Singapore, the Commonwealth, Western Australia and New Zealand; elsewhere it is an estimate from what each encoding says it covers. The pale blocks come from researched counts of the Acts in force and their length, and many of those counts are estimates; an estimate is hatched, as is an Act Commons holds without its text. The map counts Acts only. The figures above and the cards below count every instrument, in the four jurisdictions that have pages here.

How to read Commons

Every instrument in Commons moves through three stages:

  • Indexed: Commons knows the instrument exists and where its official text is published.
  • Sourced: the official text is deposited in Commons, with its licence and provenance.
  • Encoded: some or all of its rules are written in L4, with worked cases that test them.

Each jurisdiction has its own page and statute map. The addresses follow one pattern: /commons/<jurisdiction>/<type>/<name>, for example /commons/au-wa/act/wills-act-1970. A jurisdiction is a country code (au, sg, nz) or a country and state code (au-wa), the form the Akoma Ntoso legislative standard uses; Commonwealth law is at /commons/au, Western Australian law at /commons/au-wa.

Contribute

Encode a provision, verify an encoding against its source, or nominate a statute.

Every encoding in Commons is a draft, and none has yet been verified against its source by a named reviewer. The figures show how much of the statute book is in executable form, not how much is verified. The library is open source at github.com/legalese/commons.
