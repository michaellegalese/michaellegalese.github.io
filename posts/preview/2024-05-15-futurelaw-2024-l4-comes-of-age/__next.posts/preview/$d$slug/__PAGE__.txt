9:T4db,<p>In April 2024, SMU CCLAW co-organised a two-day workshop at Stanford's CodeX FutureLaw Conference on computable contracts in insurance and finance. Meng Weng Wong presented technical updates to L4 since 2023, including advances in the toolchain: an L4 specification can now be compiled to a decision service API, a natural language explanation of any decision, and a visualisation of the decision logic for non-technical review.</p>
<p>The insurance use cases were the most concrete. Claims triage and underwriting eligibility are structurally rule application problems that L4 handles naturally. The more interesting discussion was about regulatory compliance — regulators are increasingly asking insurers to demonstrate that their automated systems apply rules correctly and consistently. L4's encoded rules can be inspected directly, test cases can verify specific outcomes, and audit trails can be generated for every decision.</p>
<p>The conversation at FutureLaw has shifted from "can AI help with law?" to "which approach is right for which problem?" That is a more useful question, and one where L4's answer is clear: probabilistic models for language, symbolic systems for rule application, neurosymbolic when you need both.</p>
