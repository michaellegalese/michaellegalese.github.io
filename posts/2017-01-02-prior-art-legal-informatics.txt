<h2>(at least) 70 years of legal informatics</h2>
<p><em>The legal informatics industry is older than most web developers today.</em></p>
<p>An (incomplete) history in rough chronology—<a href="mailto:support@legalese.com?subject=Suggested%20listings%20for%20the%20Legalese%20website">let us know</a> if we missed something.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Formalising Contracts</h2>
<ul>
<li>The urge to formalize goes back at least as far as <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_of_human_thought">Leibniz</a>, that's the early 18th century</li>
<li>Lee Loevinger's <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/29762866?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents">Jurimetrics -- The Next Step Forward</a>, 1948: <em>"apparently, it has never occurred to the professional disciples of the subject that the trouble with law is not the public but the lawyers, that what is needed is not publicity but progress"</em></li>
<li>Layman E. Allen in 1956, <a href="http://digitalcommons.law.yale.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5523&#x26;context=fss_papers">Symbolic Logic: A Razor-Edged Tool for Drafting and Interpreting Legal Documents</a></li>
<li><a href="https://books.google.com/books?id=3Cc9AAAAIAAJ">Computer Science and Law</a>, 1980 where every chapter seems to have become the underpin of at least one legaltech startup</li>
<li><a href="https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:ms585mf7546/ms585mf7546.pdf">Some Speculation About Artificial Intelligence and Legal Reasoning</a> by Bruce G Buchanan &#x26; Thomas E Headrick in 1970</li>
<li>In the 1980s, the <a href="http://opim.wharton.upenn.edu/~sok/papers/s/p370-sergot.pdf">British Nationality Act was encoded in Prolog</a></li>
<li><a href="https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/0256/614d1dc55962708db9b791f70a29370f1d76.pdf">The Role of Common Ontology in Achieving Sharable, Reusable Knowledge Bases</a> by Thomas R Gruber in 1991</li>
<li>The <a href="https://link.springer.com/journal/10506">Artificial Intelligence and Law Journal</a> was founded in 1992</li>
<li><a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2FBF00118479?LI=true">Isomorphism and legal knowledge based systems</a> by TJM Bench-Capon &#x26; F P Coenen in 1992</li>
<li><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempto_Controlled_English">Attempto Controlled English</a> is an example of a formal/natural hybrid language which could be a compiler target, and has been under development since 1995</li>
<li><a href="http://executable-english.com/">Executable English</a></li>
<li><a href="http://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/idea.html">Nick Szabo, Smart Contracts</a>, 1997</li>
<li><a href="http://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0106005.pdf">The Representation of Legal Contracts</a> by Daskalopulu &#x26; Sergot, 1997</li>
<li><a href="https://www.lexifi.com/files/resources/MLFiPaper.pdf">Composing Contracts: An Adventure in Financial Engineering</a>, 2000 by Simon Peyton Jones, Jean Marc-Eber, and Julian Seward</li>
<li>Nick Szabo on <a href="http://nakamotoinstitute.org/contract-language/">A Formal Language for Analysing Contracts</a>, a preliminary draft from 2002</li>
<li><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_Business_Vocabulary_and_Business_Rules">Semantics of Business Vocabulary and Business Rules</a> is a hybrid formal/natural language for business operation and compliance which could also be a compiler target; the RFP was issued in 2003, and v.1.0 of the formal specification was released in 2008</li>
<li><a href="http://iang.org/papers/ricardian_contract.html">The Ricardian Contract</a>, 2004 relates legal language with executable code via parameterization</li>
<li>The <a href="http://wiki.ruleml.org/index.php/RuleML_Home">RuleML Initiative</a> has been collaborating with OASIS on Legal XML, Policy RuleML, LegalRuleML, and related efforts since 2004; see <a href="https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/ebca/1f32ac887a975b60552920b9b1be619a87a2.pdf">Modelling Contracts Using RuleML</a></li>
<li><a href="https://www.lexifi.com/files/resources/TimeTravel.pdf">Structured Products and Time Travel</a>, 2004 reviews potential solutions for overcoming the challenges of valuing structured products on past, present, and future dates</li>
<li><a href="https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/bitstream/10044/1/529/1/Using%20the%20event%20calculus.pdf">Using the Event Calculus for Tracking the Normative State of Contracts</a> by Andrew D. H. Farrell, Mark J. Sergot, Mathias Sallé, Claudio Bartolini, 2005</li>
<li><a href="https://www.lexifi.com/files/resources/MLFiWhitePaper.pdf">Structuring, Pricing, and Processing Complex Financial Products with MLFi</a>, 2005 described MLFi concepts and applications</li>
<li><a href="http://rbsla.ruleml.org/docs/37910209.pdf">ContractLog</a>: An Approach to Rule Based Monitoring and Execution of Service Level Agreements by Adrian Paschke, Martin Bichler, and Jens Dietrich, 2005</li>
<li><a href="http://richard.myweb.cs.uwindsor.ca/PUBLICATIONS/NLI_LFP_SURVEY_DRAFT.pdf">Realization of Natural Language Interfaces Using Lazy Functional Programming</a> by RA Frost, 2006</li>
<li><a href="http://www.cse.chalmers.se/~gersch/slides-talks/slides-CL-ModelChecking.pdf">Towards a Formal Language for Electronic Contracts</a>, 2007 describes CL</li>
<li><a href="http://www.cse.chalmers.se/~gersch/icail09.pdf">Abstract Specification of Legal Contracts</a>, Prisacariu &#x26; Schneider, 2009</li>
<li><a href="http://folk.uio.no/gerardo/ifm2009.pdf">Challenges in the Specification of Full Contracts</a>, GJ Pace and G Schneider, 2009</li>
<li><a href="http://www.nap.edu/catalog/12841.html">Need for a Common Language for All Securities and Financial Contracts</a>, 2010 by the National Research Council</li>
<li><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2658">A Software Tool for Legal Drafting</a> by Daniel Gorin, Sergio Mera, and Fernando Schapachnik, 2011</li>

<h2>Formalising Regulations</h2>
<p>As the richly interpretable saying goes, "<a href="http://courses.csail.mit.edu/iap/lawiscode/">Law is Code</a>" and, conversely, <a href="http://harvardmagazine.com/2000/01/code-is-law-html">Code is Law</a> (Lessig, 2000). Legislative and regulatory constraints may be modeled using a declarative language.</p>
<ul>
<li>In 1986, Martin Sergot et al explored <a href="http://opim.wharton.upenn.edu/~sok/papers/s/p370-sergot.pdf">The British Nationality Act as a Logic Program</a></li>

<h2>Formal Verification of Smart Contracts</h2>
<p>Formal methods and languages like <a href="http://dl.acm.org/citation.cfm?id=2841316">Agda</a>, <a href="http://cufp.org/2014/t5-edwin-brady-idris-practical-software-verification-with-Dependent-types.html">Idris</a>, <a href="http://why3.lri.fr/">Why3</a>, and <a href="https://coq.inria.fr/">CoQ</a>, not to mention specification languages like <a href="http://formalmethods.wikia.com/wiki/Z_notation">Z</a>, hold much promise. On the spectrum of software reliability, smart contracts ought to live near mission-critical or life-critical software – cryptosystems, human-crewed spacecraft, hospital equipment. Today, they're closer to enterprise web-apps.</p>
<p>See <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/09/saving-the-world-from-code/540393/">The Atlantic</a> on why formal verification is important.</p>

<li><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/ethereum/comments/4opjov/the_bug_which_the_dao_hacker_exploited_was_not/">The bug which the "DAO hacker" exploited was not "merely in the DAO itself"</a></li>
