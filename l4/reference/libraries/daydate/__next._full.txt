1e:T2c4a,<h1>daydate</h1>
<p>Dates for deadlines and time-based rules: building dates, adding days, months and years, weekdays, ISO weeks and leap years. Import it with <code>IMPORT daydate</code>. Weeks start on Monday, following ISO 8601.</p>
<p>Most functions take either a <code>DATE</code> or a day number (the date's serial number, as given by <code>Day</code>), so <code>`Weekday of` (Date 1 1 2024)</code> and <code>`Weekday of` 739250</code> give the same answer.</p>
<p><strong>Example:</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-l4-file">-- Daydate Example: Date calculations and temporal logic
IMPORT daydate

-- Date construction from components
DECIDE `Christmas 2024` IS Date 25 12 2024
DECIDE `New Year 2025` IS Date 1 1 2025

#EVAL `Christmas 2024`
#EVAL `New Year 2025`

-- Date from ISO string (returns MAYBE DATE, ignores time/timezone if present)
DECIDE `from iso date` IS Date "2025-06-15"
DECIDE `from iso datetime` IS Date "2025-06-15T10:30:00Z"
#EVAL `from iso date`       -- JUST OF (DATE OF 15, 6, 2025)
#EVAL `from iso datetime`   -- JUST OF (DATE OF 15, 6, 2025)

-- Date arithmetic
DECIDE `week later` IS `Christmas 2024` PLUS 7
#EVAL `week later`  -- January 1, 2025

DECIDE `week before` IS `Christmas 2024` MINUS 7
#EVAL `week before`  -- December 18, 2024

-- Date components
#EVAL DATE_DAY `Christmas 2024`    -- 25
#EVAL DATE_MONTH `Christmas 2024`  -- 12
#EVAL DATE_YEAR `Christmas 2024`   -- 2024

-- Weekday calculations
DECIDE `day of week` IS `Weekday of` `Christmas 2024`
#EVAL `day of week`  -- Wednesday (3)

#EVAL `Name of weekday` `Christmas 2024`  -- "Wednesday"

-- Weekend checks
#EVAL `is weekend` `Christmas 2024`   -- FALSE
#EVAL `is weekday` `Christmas 2024`   -- TRUE

DECIDE `last Saturday in 2024` IS Date 28 12 2024
#EVAL `is weekend` `last Saturday in 2024` -- TRUE

-- Week of year
#EVAL `Week of the year` `Christmas 2024`  -- 52

-- Leap year
#EVAL `is leap year` 2024  -- TRUE
#EVAL `is leap year` 2023  -- FALSE

-- Relative time phrases
DECIDE `tomorrow` IS `the day after` `Christmas 2024`
#EVAL `tomorrow`

DECIDE `next week` IS `the week after` `Christmas 2024`
#EVAL `next week`


-- Date comparison (via operator overloads)
#EVAL `Christmas 2024` &#x3C; `New Year 2025`   -- TRUE
#EVAL `Christmas 2024` >= `New Year 2025`  -- FALSE

-- Readable comparisons
DECIDE `christmas is before new year` IS `Christmas 2024` `is before` `New Year 2025`
DECIDE `new year is after christmas` IS `New Year 2025` `is after` `Christmas 2024`
#EVAL `christmas is before new year`  -- TRUE
#EVAL `new year is after christmas`   -- TRUE

-- Month helpers
DECIDE `July 4th` IS July 4 2024
#EVAL `July 4th`
#EVAL `Name of month` `July 4th`  -- "July"
</code></pre>

<p><code>add months</code> and <code>add years</code> round a fractional number down. When the day does not exist in the month they land in, they use that month's last day, as Excel's <code>EDATE</code> does: 31 January plus one month is 29 February in 2024 and 28 February in 2023, and 29 February 2024 plus one year is 28 February 2025. Use <code>add years</code> for an anniversary, since <code>Date 29 2 2025</code> rolls forward to 1 March.</p>
<h2 id="comparing-dates">Comparing dates</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>=</code>, <code>&#x3C;</code>, <code>&#x3C;=</code>, <code>></code>, <code>>=</code> and their word forms (<code>EQUALS</code>, <code>LESS THAN</code>, <code>AT MOST</code>, <code>GREATER THAN</code>, <code>AT LEAST</code>) compare two dates.</li>
<li>date1 <code>MINUS</code> date2 is the number of days between them.</li>
<li>d1 <code>`is before`</code> d2 and d1 <code>`is after`</code> d2 read as sentences. Put each date in parentheses: <code>(Date 10 1 2024) `is before` (Date 11 1 2024)</code>.</li>
<li><code>`the earlier of`</code> d1 d2 and <code>`the later of`</code> d1 d2 pick one of two dates.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="date-compat">date-compat</h2>
<p><code>date-compat</code> is a separate library for files written before <code>DATE</code> became a built-in type. Import it with <code>IMPORT `date-compat` </code> (the backticks are needed because of the hyphen). <code>daydate</code> uses it internally, but importing <code>daydate</code> does not make its functions available to you.</p>
