3:T2541,<h1>Fix wording with @nlg</h1>
<p>L4 never rewrites your words, so a rule can read badly in a rendered document.
This page shows the fix for each problem.</p>
<p><strong>Before you start:</strong> <a href="/l4/tutorials/natural-language-functions/optimising-natural-language-generation">Make rules read like law</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Examples:</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-l4-file">-- Rules that render badly. fix-wording-after.l4 is the same file with the fixes.

IMPORT daydate

DECLARE Employee
    HAS `the start date`               IS A NUMBER
        `I am or have been married`    IS A BOOLEAN
        `a member of a pension scheme` IS A BOOLEAN
        `the section engaged`          IS A NUMBER
        `is on leave`                  IS A BOOLEAN

ASSUME `is aged 16 or over` IS A FUNCTION FROM Employee TO BOOLEAN

GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`is authorised` MEANS `the employee`'s `the start date` AT LEAST 1

GIVEN `the section` IS A NUMBER
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`is one of sections 9 to 14` MEANS
    `the section` AT LEAST 9 AND `the section` AT MOST 14

GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`may opt out` MEANS
      `is authorised` `the employee`
  AND `is aged 16 or over` `the employee`
  AND NOT `the employee`'s `I am or have been married`
  AND NOT `the employee`'s `a member of a pension scheme`
  AND NOT `the employee`'s `is on leave`
  AND NOT (`is one of sections 9 to 14` (`the employee`'s `the section engaged`) AND `is authorised` `the employee`)

GIVEN last IS A DATE, next IS A DATE, agrees IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
DECIDE `the change is at least 92 days after the last, last` last `, next` next `, employer agrees:` agrees IF
    agrees OR Day next MINUS Day last AT LEAST 92
</code></pre>
<p>and</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4-file">-- The rules of fix-wording-before.l4, with their wording fixed.

IMPORT daydate

DECLARE Employee
    HAS `the start date`               IS A NUMBER
          @nlg start date
        `I am or have been married`    IS A BOOLEAN
          @nlg the employee is or has been married
        `a member of a pension scheme` IS A BOOLEAN
          @nlg the employee is a member of a pension scheme
        `the section engaged`          IS A NUMBER
          @nlg section engaged
        `is aged 16 or over`           IS A BOOLEAN
        `is on leave`                  IS A BOOLEAN

GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the employee`% is authorised
`is authorised` MEANS `the employee`'s `the start date` AT LEAST 1

GIVEN `the section` IS A NUMBER
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the section`% is one of sections 9 to 14
`is one of sections 9 to 14` MEANS
    `the section` AT LEAST 9 AND `the section` AT MOST 14

GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the employee`% is not on leave
`is at work` MEANS NOT `the employee`'s `is on leave`

GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`may opt out` MEANS
      `is authorised` `the employee`
  AND `the employee`'s `is aged 16 or over`
  AND NOT `the employee`'s `I am or have been married`
  AND NOT `the employee`'s `a member of a pension scheme`
  AND `is at work` `the employee`
  AND NOT (`is one of sections 9 to 14` (`the employee`'s `the section engaged`) AND `is authorised` `the employee`)

GIVEN `the last change` IS A DATE, `the next change` IS A DATE, `the employer agrees` IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the next change`% is at least 92 days after %`the last change`%, or %`the employer agrees`%
DECIDE `the change is far enough apart` `the last change` `the next change` `the employer agrees` IF
    `the employer agrees` OR Day `the next change` MINUS Day `the last change` AT LEAST 92
</code></pre>
<p>. See each one with <code>l4 render fix-wording-before.l4 --format text</code>.</p>

<h2 id="give-the-rule-a-subject">Give the rule a subject</h2>
<p>A rule with no sentence reads as its name, then its inputs:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text">(a) is authorised for the employee;
(f) it is not the case that both is one of sections 9 to 14 for the employee's the section engaged and …
</code></pre>
<p>Write a sentence, with a slot for each input:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the employee`% is authorised
`is authorised` MEANS `the employee`'s `the start date` AT LEAST 1
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">(a) the employee is authorised;
(f) it is not the case that both the employee's section engaged is one of sections 9 to 14 and …
</code></pre>
<h2 id="write-a-field-as-a-statement">Write a field as a statement</h2>
<p>A <code>BOOLEAN</code> field that doesn't start with <em>is</em> or <em>has</em> reads as its own words, then says whose it is:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text">(c) it is not the case that I am or have been married, for the employee;
(d) it is not the case that a member of a pension scheme, for the employee;
</code></pre>
<p>Give the field a whole statement. It then reads alone:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">`I am or have been married`    IS A BOOLEAN
  @nlg the employee is or has been married
`a member of a pension scheme` IS A BOOLEAN
  @nlg the employee is a member of a pension scheme
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">(c) it is not the case that the employee is or has been married;
(d) it is not the case that the employee is a member of a pension scheme;
</code></pre>
<h2 id="drop-the-extra-the">Drop the extra "the"</h2>
<p>Any other field reads after its owner, so a name that starts with "the" doubles up:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text">the employee's the start date
</code></pre>
<p>Give the field the words that follow the owner:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">`the start date` IS A NUMBER
  @nlg start date
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">the employee's start date
</code></pre>
<h2 id="make-an-assumed-fact-a-field">Make an assumed fact a field</h2>
<p>An <code>ASSUME</code>d function has no named inputs, so a sentence has no slot to fill:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">ASSUME `is aged 16 or over` IS A FUNCTION FROM Employee TO BOOLEAN
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">(b) is aged 16 or over with the employee;
</code></pre>
<p>Make it a <code>BOOLEAN</code> field. A field that starts with <em>is</em> or <em>has</em> follows its owner, so it needs no <code>@nlg</code>:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">DECLARE Employee
    HAS `is aged 16 or over` IS A BOOLEAN
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">(b) the employee is aged 16 or over;
</code></pre>
<h2 id="write-a-sentence-for-the-heading">Write a sentence for the heading</h2>
<p>A mixfix rule reads as its pattern, with its inputs as blanks. Punctuation in the pattern reads as written:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text">The change is at least 92 days after the last, last [last], next [next], employer agrees: [agrees] if:
</code></pre>
<p>Write a sentence, and name the inputs as nouns:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">GIVEN `the last change` IS A DATE, `the next change` IS A DATE, `the employer agrees` IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the next change`% is at least 92 days after %`the last change`%, or %`the employer agrees`%
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">[the next change] is at least 92 days after [the last change], or [the employer agrees] if:
</code></pre>
<h2 id="write-the-negative-yourself">Write the negative yourself</h2>
<p><code>NOT</code> stays outside the statement it denies:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text">(e) it is not the case that the employee is on leave; and
</code></pre>
<p>For a negative wording, write a rule with a sentence, and call it:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">GIVEN `the employee` IS AN Employee
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
@nlg %`the employee`% is not on leave
`is at work` MEANS NOT `the employee`'s `is on leave`
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text">(e) the employee is not on leave; and
</code></pre>
<h2 id="check-the-sentence">Check the sentence</h2>
<p><code>@nlg</code> changes the wording, not the logic, and L4 can't tell if a sentence is true.
Read each sentence against its rule, and try names first: a better name needs no sentence.</p>
<p>For the exact rules, see <a href="/l4/reference/syntax/nlg">@nlg</a>.</p>
