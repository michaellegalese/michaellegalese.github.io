1f:T580b,<h1>Encode legislation</h1>
<p>Turn a legal provision into L4 that mirrors the text, test it, and use an AI assistant to draft larger pieces.</p>
<p><strong>Before you start:</strong> <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/first-l4-file">Install L4 and write your first rule</a> and <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/testing-your-rules">Test your rules</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Complete example:</strong></p>
<pre><code class="language-l4-file">-- encoding-legislation-example.l4
-- Section 3 of the (imaginary) Alcohol Act 2024: who must not sell alcohol.

§ `Imaginary Alcohol Act 2024, section 3`

{-
Section 3. A person must not sell alcohol if:
(a) the person is a body corporate;
(b) the person engages in business for profit;
(c) the person is not a public house or hotel; and
(d) any of the following applies:
    (i)   the person has an unspent conviction for fraud;
    (ii)  the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information; or
    (iii) the person has an alcohol banning order.
-}

GIVEN `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a public house`                                              IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
DECIDE `the person must not sell alcohol` IF
        `is a commercial enterprise`          -- (a) and (b)
    AND NOT `is an exempt establishment`      -- (c)
    AND `has a disqualifying factor`          -- (d)
    WHERE
        `is a commercial enterprise` MEANS
                `the person is a body corporate`
            AND `the person engages in business for profit`

        `is an exempt establishment` MEANS
                `the person is a public house`
            OR  `the person is a hotel`

        `has a disqualifying factor` MEANS
                `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`
            OR  `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information`
            OR  `the person has an alcohol banning order`

§§ `Tests`

-- A corporate pub is exempt under (c), even with a fraud conviction.
#ASSERT NOT `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS TRUE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS TRUE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS TRUE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS FALSE

-- A company with a banning order is prohibited.
#ASSERT `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS TRUE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS FALSE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS TRUE

-- A company with no disqualifying factor under (d) is not prohibited.
#ASSERT NOT `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS TRUE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS FALSE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS FALSE

-- A natural person is not covered by (a).
#EVAL `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS FALSE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS FALSE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS TRUE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS FALSE
</code></pre>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-provision">The provision</h2>
<p>We will encode this section of an imaginary Alcohol Act:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Section 3.</strong> A person must not sell alcohol if:
(a) the person is a body corporate;
(b) the person engages in business for profit;
(c) the person is not a public house or hotel; and
(d) any of the following applies:
(i) the person has an unspent conviction for fraud;
(ii) the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information; or
(iii) the person has an alcohol banning order.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-1-analyse-the-structure">Step 1: Analyse the structure</h2>
<p>Before writing code, find:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The outcome</strong>: "must not sell alcohol".</li>
<li><strong>The conditions</strong>: (a), (b), (c) and (d), which must all hold.</li>
<li><strong>The alternatives</strong>: (d)(i), (d)(ii) and (d)(iii), of which any one is enough.</li>
</ol>
<p>The logical shape is:</p>
<pre><code>(a) AND (b) AND (c) AND ((d)(i) OR (d)(ii) OR (d)(iii))
</code></pre>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-2-write-the-rule">Step 2: Write the rule</h2>
<p>Each fact the section depends on becomes an input, named in the words of the statute. Each condition becomes one line, with a comment pointing to its paragraph:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">§ `Imaginary Alcohol Act 2024, section 3`

GIVEN `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a public house`                                              IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
DECIDE `the person must not sell alcohol` IF
        `the person is a body corporate`                                              -- (a)
    AND `the person engages in business for profit`                                   -- (b)
    AND NOT (`the person is a public house` OR `the person is a hotel`)               -- (c)
    AND (    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                         -- (d)(i)
         OR  `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` -- (d)(ii)
         OR  `the person has an alcohol banning order`)                               -- (d)(iii)
</code></pre>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Legal text</th>
<th>L4</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>"the person is a body corporate"</td>
<td><code>`the person is a body corporate`</code></td>
</tr>

</tr>

</tr>

<p>The facts are <code>GIVEN</code> inputs, so a caller, a test or a deployed API supplies them. If you prefer to declare them once at the top of a file, see <a href="/l4/reference/types/ASSUME">ASSUME</a>.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-3-test-it">Step 3: Test it</h2>
<p>Add tests that state the expected outcome. A rule can be called with its inputs named, <code>rule WITH `input` IS value</code>, one input per line, which keeps long test cases readable:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">-- A corporate pub is exempt under (c), even with a fraud conviction.
#ASSERT NOT `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS TRUE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS TRUE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS TRUE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS FALSE

-- A company with a banning order is prohibited.
#ASSERT `the person must not sell alcohol` WITH
    `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS TRUE
    `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS TRUE
    `the person is a public house`                                              IS FALSE
    `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS FALSE
    `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS FALSE
    `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS TRUE
</code></pre>
<p><code>l4 run</code> prints <code>assertion satisfied</code> for each. Use <code>#ASSERT</code> (or <code>#EVAL</code> and compare the result yourself), not <code>#CHECK</code>: <code>#CHECK</code> only type-checks and never evaluates the rule.</p>
<p>Cover at least:</p>
<ul>
<li>each exemption on its own;</li>
<li>each disqualifying factor on its own;</li>
<li>the case where nothing in (d) applies;</li>
<li>a person outside the section altogether, for example not a body corporate.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-4-name-the-parts">Step 4: Name the parts</h2>
<p>For longer provisions, give each part of the test a name. <code>WHERE</code> defines the parts after the rule, so the main condition reads like a summary of the section:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4">GIVEN `the person is a body corporate`                                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person engages in business for profit`                                 IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a public house`                                              IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person is a hotel`                                                     IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`                            IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information` IS A BOOLEAN
      `the person has an alcohol banning order`                                   IS A BOOLEAN
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
DECIDE `the person must not sell alcohol` IF
        `is a commercial enterprise`          -- (a) and (b)
    AND NOT `is an exempt establishment`      -- (c)
    AND `has a disqualifying factor`          -- (d)
    WHERE
        `is a commercial enterprise` MEANS
                `the person is a body corporate`
            AND `the person engages in business for profit`

        `is an exempt establishment` MEANS
                `the person is a public house`
            OR  `the person is a hotel`

        `has a disqualifying factor` MEANS
                `the person has an unspent conviction for fraud`
            OR  `the person has an unspent conviction for providing misleading information`
            OR  `the person has an alcohol banning order`
</code></pre>
<p>The inputs and the tests stay the same, so the tests from Step 3 confirm that the refactored rule still means the same thing.</p>

<h2 id="tips">Tips</h2>
<p><strong>Keep the statute's words.</strong> <code>`the person is a body corporate`</code> lets a lawyer check the rule against the text; <code>isCompany</code> does not.</p>
<p><strong>Read "or" carefully.</strong> In legislation, "A, B or C" usually means any one or more of them: <code>A OR B OR C</code>.</p>
<p><strong>Watch negations.</strong> "Not a public house or hotel" normally means neither: <code>NOT (pub OR hotel)</code>. The other reading, <code>NOT pub OR NOT hotel</code>, is true for almost everyone and is rarely intended. When the text is ambiguous, write down which reading you chose, in a comment or a test.</p>
<p><strong>Keep the source next to the code.</strong> The complete example puts the text of section 3 in a <code>{- ... -}</code> comment above the rule and marks each condition with its paragraph number.</p>

<h2 id="drafting-with-an-ai-assistant">Drafting with an AI assistant</h2>
<p>An AI assistant can draft L4 from legislation quickly, but it can also misread the law. Use it for the first draft, and let the type checker and a human reviewer decide whether the draft is right. In VS Code, Legalese AI already knows L4 syntax and sees the type checker's errors after every edit; see <a href="/l4/tutorials/llm-integration/composing-l4-with-ai">Draft rules with Legalese AI</a>. The prompts below work with any assistant.</p>
<h3 id="the-workflow">The workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Segment.</strong> Split the legislation into self-contained pieces: one article or section each, with its number, and with the definitions it uses.</li>
<li><strong>Extract.</strong> Ask the assistant for the types and inputs the piece needs.</li>
<li><strong>Draft.</strong> Ask for the L4 rule.</li>
<li><strong>Check.</strong> Run <code>l4 check</code>, then the tests, then review the draft against the text.</li>
<li><strong>Iterate.</strong> Give the errors and your review notes back to the assistant.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="prompt-extract-types">Prompt: extract types</h3>
<pre><code>You are analysing legislation to encode it in L4, a programming language
for law.

Legislation:
"""
Article 2 - Interpretation
In this Law -
"charitable purpose" means any of the purposes specified in Schedule 1;
"charity" means an entity that is established for charitable purposes only;
"governor" means any person who is responsible for the control and
management of the administration of a registered charity;
"""

List every defined term that should become a type. For each, say:
1. its name;
2. whether it is a fixed list of choices or a record with fields;
3. its choices or fields, with their types;
4. which other types or provisions it refers to.
</code></pre>
<h3 id="prompt-draft-the-rule">Prompt: draft the rule</h3>
<pre><code>Write L4 for the provision below.

L4 basics:
- Records: DECLARE Charity HAS `purposes` IS A LIST OF Purpose
- Choices: DECLARE Purpose IS ONE OF `relief of poverty`, `advancement of education`
- Rules: GIVEN `the entity` IS A Charity
         GIVETH A BOOLEAN
         DECIDE `meets the charity test` IF ... AND ...
- Names with spaces go in backticks; fields are read with 's.
- List functions such as all, any, map and filter need IMPORT prelude.

Provision:
"""
Article 5 - Charity test
An entity meets the charity test if -
(a) all of its purposes are charitable purposes; and
(b) it satisfies the public benefit requirement.
"""

Types already defined:
- Charity, with `purposes` (a LIST OF Purpose)
- Purpose, a list of the charitable purposes

Use the words of the provision as names. Mark each condition with a
comment giving its paragraph, and add #ASSERT tests for each paragraph.
</code></pre>
<h3 id="prompt-fix-an-error">Prompt: fix an error</h3>
<p>Paste the checker's message exactly as <code>l4 check</code> printed it:</p>
<pre><code>l4 check reported this error:

    I could not find a definition for the identifier

      Purpose

The types defined in the file are CharitablePurpose and Charity.
Fix the draft and explain the change.
</code></pre>

<h3 id="review-checklist">Review checklist</h3>
<ul>
<li>Types and names match the definitions in the legislation.</li>
<li>Every condition in the text appears in the rule, and nothing else does.</li>
<li>Cross-references point to the right provisions.</li>
<li>There are tests for each paragraph and for the edges (thresholds, empty lists, exemptions).</li>
<li><code>l4 check</code> passes and every <code>#ASSERT</code> is satisfied.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Example:</strong> a reviewed draft of a governor-disqualification article, with its tests:</p>
<pre><code class="language-l4-file">-- governor-disqualification-example.l4
-- A reviewed AI draft of Article 19(1) of the Charities (Jersey) Law 2014.

IMPORT prelude

§ `Article 19 - Disqualification of governors`

{-
(1) A person is disqualified from acting as a governor of a charity if -
    (a) the person is an undischarged bankrupt;
    (b) the person has an unspent conviction for an offence involving
        dishonesty or deception;
    (c) the person is disqualified from acting as a company director; or
    (d) the person is subject to a disqualification order under Article 23.
-}

DECLARE Conviction
    HAS `offence`                               IS A STRING
        `is spent`                              IS A BOOLEAN
        `involves dishonesty or deception`      IS A BOOLEAN

DECLARE Person
    HAS `name`                                  IS A STRING
        `is an undischarged bankrupt`           IS A BOOLEAN
        `convictions`                           IS A LIST OF Conviction
        `is disqualified as a company director` IS A BOOLEAN
        `disqualification orders`               IS A LIST OF STRING

-- Article 19(1)(b)
GIVEN `the person` IS A Person
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`has an unspent conviction for dishonesty` MEANS
    any (GIVEN `the conviction` YIELD
                `the conviction`'s `involves dishonesty or deception`
            AND NOT `the conviction`'s `is spent`)
        (`the person`'s `convictions`)

-- Article 19(1)
GIVEN `the person` IS A Person
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
DECIDE `is disqualified from acting as a governor` IF
        `the person`'s `is an undischarged bankrupt`              -- (a)
    OR  `has an unspent conviction for dishonesty` `the person`   -- (b)
    OR  `the person`'s `is disqualified as a company director`    -- (c)
    OR  NOT null (`the person`'s `disqualification orders`)       -- (d)

§§ `Tests`

`Dana` MEANS Person WITH
    `name`                                  IS "Dana"
    `is an undischarged bankrupt`           IS FALSE
    `convictions`                           IS LIST Conviction "theft" FALSE TRUE
    `is disqualified as a company director` IS FALSE
    `disqualification orders`               IS EMPTY

`Eli` MEANS Person WITH
    `name`                                  IS "Eli"
    `is an undischarged bankrupt`           IS FALSE
    `convictions`                           IS LIST Conviction "theft" TRUE TRUE
    `is disqualified as a company director` IS FALSE
    `disqualification orders`               IS EMPTY

-- (b): an unspent conviction for theft disqualifies.
#ASSERT `is disqualified from acting as a governor` `Dana`

-- A spent conviction does not.
#ASSERT NOT `is disqualified from acting as a governor` `Eli`
</code></pre>
<h3 id="keep-a-record">Keep a record</h3>
<p>Commit the source text, the prompts and the L4 together, and note which assistant produced the draft. <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/version-control-for-rules">Manage rules in git and CI</a> shows how.</p>
<p>An assistant can misread a legal structure, miss a cross-reference, or invent an interpretation. A draft that type-checks can still be wrong: only the tests and the review show whether it says what the law says.</p>
