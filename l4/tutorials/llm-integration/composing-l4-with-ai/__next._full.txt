1f:T1ddf,<h1>Draft rules with Legalese AI</h1>
<p>Go from a policy or contract in plain language to a checked L4 draft, with Legalese AI in VS Code.</p>
<p><strong>Before you start:</strong> <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/first-l4-file">Install L4 and write your first rule</a>.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="setup">Setup</h2>
<ol>
<li>Install the <a href="https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=Legalese.l4-vscode">L4 VS Code extension</a>.</li>
<li>Open a folder for your rules in VS Code. Putting it under git from the start is a good idea; see <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/version-control-for-rules">Manage rules in git and CI</a>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>L4</strong> sidebar, sign in with Legalese Cloud, and switch to the <strong>Legalese AI</strong> tab. Say "Hello" to check that it answers.</li>
</ol>
<p>Legalese AI already knows L4 syntax and how to deploy, so you don't need to paste a cheat sheet into your prompts.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-1-frame-the-source-text">Step 1: Frame the source text</h2>
<p>Before prompting, decide three things:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Scope</strong>: one clause, one section, or the whole document? Smaller pieces give cleaner drafts.</li>
<li><strong>The decision</strong>: what question should the rule answer? For example <code>`is eligible for refund`</code>, <code>`amount owed`</code> or <code>`is in breach`</code>.</li>
<li><strong>The inputs</strong>: what facts will a caller provide? A person, an order, a claim?</li>
</ol>
<p>Write these down as a short brief:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> Section 4 of our refund policy (pasted below).
<strong>Decision:</strong> <code>is eligible for refund</code>, giving a BOOLEAN.
<strong>Inputs:</strong> an <code>Order</code> with purchase date, amount and product category; the current date.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-2-ask-for-a-draft">Step 2: Ask for a draft</h2>
<p>Paste the brief and the source text into Legalese AI:</p>
<pre><code>Draft L4 for the refund policy below.

Brief:
- Decision: `is eligible for refund`, giving a BOOLEAN
- Input: an Order record
- Put the result in rules/refund.l4

Source (Section 4):
"""
A customer may request a refund within 30 days of purchase, provided
the product has not been opened. Digital goods are non-refundable
except where required by law. Orders over $500 require manager approval
before a refund is issued.
"""

Start with the type declarations, then the decision rule.
Mark each condition with a comment citing the source.
</code></pre>
<p>A typical draft declares <code>Order</code> and any lists of choices (such as a product category), writes the main <code>DECIDE</code> rule, splits sub-conditions into named helpers so the rule reads like the policy, and adds examples at the end.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-3-let-it-fix-errors-then-add-tests">Step 3: Let it fix errors, then add tests</h2>
<p>You don't need to ask Legalese AI to check its work. Every time it creates, edits or reads an <code>.l4</code> file, it gets the type checker's diagnostics for that file. If there are errors (a misspelt name, a wrong field access, a list where a single value was expected), it fixes them before it answers. You can watch the same diagnostics in VS Code's Problems panel.</p>
<p>When the draft is clean, ask for tests:</p>
<pre><code>Add three #EVAL blocks:
1. An opened digital good bought yesterday: expect FALSE.
2. An unopened $200 physical good bought 10 days ago: expect TRUE.
3. An unopened $900 physical good bought 10 days ago: expect it to
   need manager approval.
</code></pre>
<p>After that edit, Legalese AI sees the <code>#EVAL</code> results and can compare them with what you asked for. If a result contradicts what you expected, you have found something: the policy is ambiguous, the draft misread it, or your own reading was wrong. Each of these is worth knowing.</p>
<p>A file that type-checks is not necessarily right. The checker catches rules that don't fit together, not rules that say the wrong thing; that is what the tests and the review are for.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="step-4-review">Step 4: Review</h2>
<p>A draft is not finished when it type-checks. Review it for:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fidelity</strong>: does each clause of the source appear in the L4, and nothing else?</li>
<li><strong>Silent assumptions</strong>: did the draft decide something the source leaves open, such as what "opened" means or whether store credit counts?</li>
<li><strong>Cross-references</strong>: are other sections the policy mentions encoded, or only stubbed?</li>
<li><strong>Edge cases</strong>: a purchase on day 30 and day 31, an order of exactly $500, a zero-value order.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask Legalese AI to list its assumptions, so the policy owner can confirm them:</p>
<pre><code>List every assumption you made that is not explicit in the source text,
with a one-line justification for each.
</code></pre>
<hr>
<h2 id="a-complete-session">A complete session</h2>
<pre><code>You: Draft L4 in rules/late-fee.l4 for:
     "A late fee of 5% of the outstanding balance applies to any
     payment received more than 14 days after the due date. The fee
     is waived for first-time late payments."
     Decision: `late fee owed`, giving a NUMBER. Input: a Payment.

AI:  [writes rules/late-fee.l4 with a Payment record, helper rules
     and tests; sees a misspelt field name in the diagnostics and
     fixes it; the file checks clean]

You: Add a test for a payment 20 days late where the customer has
     two earlier late payments. Expect 5% of the balance.

AI:  [adds the test, sees its result after the edit, confirms]

You: List your assumptions.

AI:  1. "First-time" means no earlier late payment ever, not
        "first this year"; the source does not say.
     2. Days late are calendar days, not business days.
     3. The 5% applies to the balance on the due date, not on the
        payment date.
</code></pre>
<p>The policy owner now has three questions to answer. That is the point.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="tips">Tips</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One clause per prompt</strong> when the source is dense. Combine the pieces later.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the brief in a file</strong> (for example <code>rules/brief.md</code>) and refer to it instead of retyping it.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for alternatives</strong> when a clause is ambiguous: "show two encodings and explain the difference".</li>
<li><strong>Let it prepare deployment</strong>: once the file is clean, ask Legalese AI to mark the decision with <code>@export</code> and walk you through <a href="/l4/tutorials/deploying-rules/exporting-rules-for-deployment">deploying your rules</a>.</li>
<li><strong>For legislation</strong>, see <a href="/l4/tutorials/getting-started/encoding-legislation#drafting-with-an-ai-assistant">Encode legislation</a>: a workflow and prompts for statutes.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="limits">Limits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Legalese AI can misread unusual legal structures. Compare the draft with the source, clause by clause.</li>
<li>Tests it writes reflect its own reading of the source. Write your own tests before you trust the rule.</li>
<li>A rule that type-checks can still say the wrong thing.</li>
</ul>
